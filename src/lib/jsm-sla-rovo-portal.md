Atlassian has revamped **Service Level Agreement (SLA)** management in **Jira Service Management (JSM)** with two key improvements designed to streamline administrators' work and give end customers greater transparency.

At **Quabu**, we sponsor the latest **JiraBro** tutorial, which explains how to make the most of the new rules engine. Below, we summarize both what you will see in the video and the new native portal visibility feature announced by Atlassian.

## 1. New Rules Engine and SLA Creation with Atlassian Rovo (in the Video)

In the JiraBro video, you will see a live demonstration of how to configure your metrics using JSM's revamped interface, which lets you create SLAs **from scratch** or use **Atlassian Rovo** to generate rules with AI assistance. The main settings include:

- **Field conditions and goals:** Filter which tickets each metric applies to and define their **priority and target time**.
- **Precise clock control:** Configure the exact conditions for **starting, pausing and stopping** the timer to accurately reflect real service times.

## 2. New Native Feature: SLA Visibility in the Customer Portal

Atlassian has also launched *View SLA in Portal* (available on **JSM Cloud Premium and Enterprise** plans), a highly requested feature that displays estimated times directly in the customer's request view.

![Customer request view displaying SLA expectations in the Jira Service Management portal](JSM_SLA_PORTAL_IMAGE)

- **Granular control for each metric:** Space administrators decide which SLAs to enable or disable in the portal—for example, displaying *Time to first response* without changing calendars, pauses or internal reports.
- **Clear messages without internal jargon:** To avoid confusion or unnecessary pressure on agents, customers see three simple states: the **estimated target time** for that phase, **“Completed”** (once the ticket has moved beyond that stage, without revealing whether the internal target was breached), or **“Working on it”** (when no specific timeframe is available but the request is still active).
